# Starsilk canon boundaries

SILKBOUND is an explicitly **non-canon operational dramatization** in a fictional peripheral system during Years 7–120 of the Blood Eclipse War. The game keeps historically established facts separate from generated simulation data.

## Fixed canon constraints used here

- The Blood Eclipse War is fought between the Administration and the Drakken Empire under Mother's planetary will.
- Drakken are elemental terraforming entities developed through the Notebook Program. Their ordinary prewar-era/earlier-war behavior is not given the later weaponized Starsilk capability.
- Starsilk is literal programmable cosmological material. Extracting it from a star immediately causes gravitational collapse into a black hole.
- No raw Starsilk is a mineable, craftable or consumable player resource. The game gives the player no new universal immunity or Starsilk tool.
- The only established safe interaction mechanisms remain Tiger's immunity, Syrin blood's neutralizing glitch, and Codec's Syrin-blood vapor method. None is an ordinary salvage crafting recipe.
- Macros are repeatable executable reality operations, not generic magic spells.
- Blood Rings are red vitrified-glass propaganda structures, not gateways.
- Shard-God Tiger is controlled, calculating, pragmatic and cruel—not a random monster or a source of randomized powers.
- Codec is a morally compromised Administration tactical/data authority in this period.
- The Siege Wall does not yet exist; it arises at the end of the war in Year 170. Drakken's first documented weaponized use of Starsilk comes at the end of the war.
- Death is final. A saved data fragment is not a consciously suffering ghost. SILKBOUND's death screen ends that captain's run; starting another expedition creates a new life and seed.
- The established canon character is **Kail**, never Kyle. The playable SilkBound captain is a separate fictional simulation character, not Kail or a disguised canon cameo.
- Starsilk itself is never depicted as conscious.

## Deliberate game invention

The Ashlands, Glassfall Survey and Drowned Remnant are generated, unnamed peripheral survey worlds. The civilian vessel, Iri, Vale, their circumstances, all structures, resource seams, missions and outcomes are invented for the simulation. Glassfall's bounded fault conversion models a physical terraforming consequence; it does not claim to reproduce a named canon site or establish a new Drakken strain or power. Its separate before/after geological survey entries are invented operational evidence, not historical canon. The warning markings represent geology rather than Starsilk phenomena.

The scanner's archive returns are damaged physical Administration hardware. Its records do not preserve a mind. The starship's field coil, coolant loop, suit lining and cutter are ordinary engineering equipment and do not interact with Starsilk.

In-game compendium entries are explicitly prefixed `CANON CONTEXT` or `SIMULATION RECORD`. The simulated player receives no morality score and does not save the universe.
